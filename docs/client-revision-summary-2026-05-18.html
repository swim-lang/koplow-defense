Koplow Defense

Revision Summary And Source Reconciliation

This note explains what was revised on the site, how the motion direction was interpreted, and where the revision PDF expanded on the original design files, mainly in the lower-page narrative sections rather than across the entire site structure.

Source Materials

The revision PDF and the original design files provided for the project were reviewed against the existing site build.

Short Version

We started from the original design files, and the PDF then introduced more developed direction for the lower-page homepage sequence and the related “What Happens Next” style beats near the ends of key pages.

What Was Updated

  • Motion: section transitions were refined so the site feels more deliberate and less decorative.
  • Sequencing: the main structural updates were concentrated in the lower-page beats, especially the homepage ending and the “What Happens Next” style closing sections.
  • Bio/image integration: image-to-text compositions on pages such as About and Ethics were strengthened where the feedback called for a more integrated feel.
  • CTA treatment: prominent buttons were made larger, bolder, and more proportional to the surrounding headings.
  • Responsive behavior: the site was cleaned up for phone and tablet so layout, wrapping, spacing, and touch targets hold together more reliably.

How The Motion Was Interpreted

The PDF uses terms like snap to describe how certain sections should feel, but the feedback does not appear to call for literal viewport locking or browser-style scroll capture. In other words, the section should feel like it arrives decisively, not like the browser is taking over the scroll.

A literal viewport snap was tested and proved too aggressive. It made scrolling feel trapped, especially on trackpads, and created the sense that the page was stopping the user rather than guiding the content into place.

For that reason, the current implementation interprets snap as a stronger section arrival, or a more magnetic landing, rather than a locked viewport behavior. In practice, that means the content lands more decisively under normal scrolling, but the page itself never takes control away from the user.

This part is naturally the most open to debate, because the feedback describes a feeling rather than a strict technical requirement. The current version is meant to honor the direction without creating a worse scrolling experience.

Where The Design Files And PDF Differed

The design files available for this phase contain desktop layouts for Home and About, plus menu and stylesheet references. They do not contain equivalent desktop layouts for Criminal, Ethics, or How.

That matters because the PDF gives explicit motion and sequencing direction for those pages, but there is not a matching set of desktop layouts in the provided design files to compare against one-to-one. The main difference was concentrated toward the bottoms of the pages. On the homepage in particular, the PDF called out beats that did not yet exist as standalone sections in the original source layout, so those notes had to be treated as new design direction, not only as revision markup.

  • Home design layout: originally showed Hero → How We Defend → image bridge → What Sharp Gets You → a light/gray When It Matters section → a separate dark CTA/footer section.
  • Home PDF notes: introduced a more explicit lower-page narrative sequence, including What Happens Next and a distinct decision-block moment that do not appear as standalone sections in the original home layout.
  • Practical effect: this meant the homepage notes were read not just as edits to existing blocks, but as additional directional design input for how that lower-page sequence should evolve.
  • Related lower-page sections: once that homepage sequence was clarified by the PDF, similar “What Happens Next” style beats at the ends of other key pages were interpreted through that same lower-page lens.
  • Other revisions: outside of those lower-page sequences, most of the remaining changes were more straightforward refinements such as transitions, integrated bio/image treatments, CTA scale, and responsive cleanup.
